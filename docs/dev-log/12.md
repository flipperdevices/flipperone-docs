In [Dev Log #11](./11.md), we measured how much power Flipper One spends on itself. This time, we went the other way and taught it to give power to other devices over the USB-C1 port. You choose how much it gives, up to 27W at 20V.

---

## What changed since Dev Log #2

Back in June, in [Dev Log #2](./2.md), we charged a laptop from Flipper One. That was a hardware test. Linux didn't know about it yet and offered every connected device the same 5V at 2A.

Since then, we measured what the port can do under load and wrote the software around the results. Flipper One now has three power sets from 5V to 20V and a setting in FlipCTL to choose between them. We also added protections for the devices you connect.

---

## What you can power

The 24Wh battery mainly powers Flipper One itself. Still, when there is no outlet around, it has enough to charge a phone about once, or a pair of earbuds.

The higher voltages make more things possible. You could power a USB-C soldering iron, which wants 12-20V. At 27W, it would heat up more slowly than from a wall charger, but that should be enough to fix a broken wire in the field. You could also power your DIY electronics on the go: a USB Power Delivery (PD) trigger board or cable would give you 9V, 12V, 15V, or 20V for a project you're working on.

## Three power sets

| Power set        | What it offers                                              | How long                   |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| **5V** (default) | 5V at 3A                                                    | Until the battery is empty |
| **18W**          | 5V at 3A, 9V at 2A, 12V at 1.5A, 15V at 1.1A, 20V at 0.75A  | Until the battery is empty |
| **27W**          | 5V at 3A, 9V at 3A, 12V at 2.25A, 15V at 1.8A, 20V at 1.35A | About 20 minutes           |

We reset the port to the 5V set on every reboot, and we did it on purpose: some displays that see a port offering more than 5V decide it's a charger and refuse to show a picture over USB-C.

---

## The battery temperature sets the limit

In Dev Log #2, we reported a test at 15V and 3A, which is 45W. The battery charger inside Flipper One can do that, but the battery gets too hot, so the strongest set stops at 27W.

We took a fully charged Flipper One, placed it flat on its heat sink in a 25.5 °C room, and drew 27W at 20V. In 19 minutes, the battery heated from 38 °C to 58.5 °C. At 60 °C, the battery gauge disconnects the battery.

We made the 18W set weaker, so that the battery runs empty before it gets too hot. In the hardest test, 15V at 1.1A, the battery ran empty in 59 minutes and reached 56.1 °C. The 27W set is still there for short jobs.

**Charging got safer too**

While we were at it, we fixed charging as well. The battery is rated to charge between 0 °C and 45 °C, and Flipper One now keeps to that range. If the battery got warm while powering another device, it waits to cool down before it charges again.
:::

## How we protect the connected device

With up to 20V on a USB port, we needed a plan for when things go wrong.

The worst case is Linux hanging while the port is at 20V. Linux now has to reset a timer in the charger every 250ms, and when a second passes without a reset, the charger switches the output off on its own. For the same reason, Flipper One refuses to sleep while the port gives more than 5V, because a sleeping system could miss that you moved the cable to another device.

We also made the charger discharge the line when the port steps to a lower voltage or stops giving power. Without that, the voltage falls too slowly when the connected device draws almost nothing.
