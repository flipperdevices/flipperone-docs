The Flipper One is meant to double as a [Hackers TV media box](https://blog.flipper.net/flipper-one-we-need-your-help/#hackers-tv-media-box). The idea is that you can plug it into a VP, pick the profile you need, and Kodi comes up. Over the past week, a community tester, [Aurux](https://github.com/Aurux), did exactly that. Multiple 4K monitors, two screens at once, USB-C docs, the whole gambit.

As always, drop your comments at the bottom of the post!

---

## Who gets 4k?

The Flipper One can send full 4K video to one of it's two video outputs, and users can select which one.

There's slight problem, however: where do you pick which port? Aurux did some digging. On release candidate 1276, they plugged a 4K monitor into the USB-C port and got only 1920×2160, half the monitor's width. So Kodi couldn't do 4K at all. A [workaround existed](https://github.com/flipperdevices/flipperone-linux-build-scripts/issues/194), but it only worked with HDMI unplugged.

The reason is how the Rockchip RK3576 draws video. It has three video output channels ("video ports"), and they are not equal:

- VPO: 4k up to 120hz
- VP1: 2.5k at 60hz
- VP2: Full HD (yay) at 60hz

Usually, HDMI is on VP0 and the USB-C DisplayPort is on VP1. So HDMI gets the 4K. However, if you'd prefer to have 4K over USB-C instead, the profile configuration has a switch for that. It's just a little hard to find currently, so we're moving that screen somewhere more visible.

## The BSOD is back!

A black screen at 4K and 120Hz over HDMI came down to a miscalculation. An issue was reported and fixed on the same day!

4K at 120Hz over HDMI 2.1 is one of the Flipper One's headline specs, so of course Aurux tried it next. On build 1289, with the TV Media Box profile sending 4K to HDMI, a Dell S2725QC monitor happily accepted the 4K/120Hz mode but then stayed completely black. The system logs [filled up with display errors](https://github.com/flipperdevices/flipperone-linux-build-scripts/issues/199). Dropping to 60Hz brought the picture back, and 4K/120Hz worked fine over USB-C DisplayPort, so the monitor wasn't the problem.

We asked Aurux for two diagnostic dumps: the list of display modes the system was offering, and a summary of the chip's clocks. Aurux posted both for the broken mode and the working one, and comparing them told the story:

- HDMI 2.1 uses a faster link mode for big signals like this. The driver decides whether it needs that mode by checking if the display clock is at least 600 MHz.
- The RK3576 handles two pixels per clock tick, so for this particular mode the display clock lands at 594 MHz.
- That's 6 MHz under the line, so the driver set up the wrong link and the monitor got no picture.

We fixed the check and had a test build ready the same day. Aurux tried it, confirmed it worked, and that was that.

## Multiple monitors

With two monitors connected, the TV Media Box now shows Kodi on one screen instead of splitting it across both.

Aurux also tried HDMI and USB-C DisplayPort at the same time. Kodi stretched into one giant window across both monitors, and which half landed on which screen depended on the order the cables went in. A reboot didn't help, and [playback was choppy](https://github.com/flipperdevices/flipperone-linux-build-scripts/issues/197) once it spanned both screens.

The culprit is a default setting. Kodi runs inside a minimal display program, and unless it's told to pick a screen, it just lays them all side by side. A proper TV box shows its picture on one screen, so that's what we've made it do. Kodi now uses the most recently connected screen, and the previous one takes over again when you unplug it.

## Who gets the power?

The Flipper One now charges from a USB-C dock that gets its own power after you plug it in. We deliberately didn't make it swap roles on its own, though.

Back in [Dev Log #2](https://docs.flipper.net/one/dev-log/2), we showed the Flipper One charging other USB-C devices at up to 15 V and 3 A. This time Aurux tried the opposite direction. Plenty of docks and dongles can either give or take power, so this was an sensible thing to test. If one of them gets plugged into the Flipper One first, it asks for power, so the Flipper One supplies it. Then you plug a wall charger into the dock, and some docks never announce that they now have power of their own. [Aurux's report](https://github.com/flipperdevices/flipperone-linux-build-scripts/issues/196) used a UGREEN multiport dongle, and the Flipper One just kept supplying power even with a charger connected.

The suggestion was for the Flipper One to periodically ask to swap roles. We passed on that, for two reasons: 

1. It could start charging from your phone at the worst possible moment. 
1. Two Flippers plugged into each other would swap back and forth forever, likely causing more problems.

The real fix was simpler. The night before the report, we'd changed the power options the Flipper One advertises, so both the supplier and consumer options now say _"I can do either role"_. With that in the next nightly build, Aurux's dock switched over and the Flipper One started charging.

What's still missing is a way for you to choose. We'd like to add a settings screen where you can request a role switch yourself and set a default for devices that can do both.

> **Which brings us to a question for you**: if you plug your Flipper One into something that can both charge it and be charged by it, what should happen by default? Let us know in the comments!

## Camera support is heading into mainline Linux

The driver for Flipper One's camera interface has been accepted into the standard Linux kernel, due in version 7.4.

Flipper One has a camera interface, but the mainline kernel supported it only on Rockchip's bigger RK3588 chip (not RK3576). The two chips are similar, so in May we opened [an issue](https://github.com/flipperdevices/flipper-linux-kernel/issues/6) as an open call for the community to port the RK3588 code to the RK3576. We use two data lanes from the chip's camera controller while other boards (like the ArmSoM Sige5 and the FriendlyElec NanoPi M5) use the same connection with more bandwidth. if their cameras work, ours should too, right?

Luckily, the patches were [accepted upstream for Linux 7.4](https://lore.kernel.org/linux-rockchip/179122712929.3317128.1173871776565894592.b4-ty@sntech.de/) and the issue was closed.

<!-- IMAGE: a photo of the camera interface working properly! -->

### Why this matters

As we said in [Dev Log #6](https://docs.flipper.net/one/dev-log/6), we push our changes upstream and use unpatched mainline software wherever we can. Once these patches are in the standard kernel, we no longer have to carry and re-apply them on every kernel update, and anyone running Linux 7.4 or later on an RK3576 board gets the same driver. It follows the hardware video decoding work we reported in [Dev Log #1](https://docs.flipper.net/one/dev-log/1). You can follow the rest of this effort on the [Rockchip RK3576 mainline support](https://docs.flipper.net/one/cpu-software/rk3576-mainlining) page.
