This is our Dev Log where we share how the development of Flipper One is going.

**In this Dev Log:** We take a look at the Home Screen: Design Ideas So Far.

---

![Header](/files/pics/dev-log/12/devlog12-header.png)

One of the most exciting things about developing Flipper One in the open is seeing how ideas evolve through collaboration. People from different backgrounds, with different experiences and perspectives, come together to explore possibilities and help shape the device.

A recent discussion in the Flipper One UI repository is a great example of this in action, exploring a deceptively simple question:

What should the Linux Mode home screen look like?

It's an opportunity to rethink how users interact with the device, turning the home screen into something that is not only visually distinctive but also genuinely useful.

---

## Initial Proposals

The initial proposals were deliberately open-ended. Rather than settling on a particular layout straight away, the discussion explored how the home screen could balance useful system information with a visual identity that feels unique to Linux Mode.

Some of the information being considered includes:

- CPU temperature and processor load
- Network activity and connectivity
- System uptime
- Time and date
- User-customisable information

Another interesting idea was to move away from treating the Dolphin as a standalone illustration and instead make it an integral part of the interface.

---

## Three creative directions

The open discussion has attracted some great design explorations from the community.

Triple Tres took the initial idea and developed three distinct visual directions, each designed around the limitations and possibilities of Flipper One's monochrome display.

### 1. The Jar

The first concept explores placing a simplified character inside a container, creating an opportunity to turn the surrounding environment into part of the interface.

Adam Rzepecki suggested a particularly interesting idea: using the fluid level inside the jar as a visual indicator for things like battery level or system temperature.

Taking this a step further, the Flipper Dolphin could even have its own animations, perhaps starting to sweat when things get a little too hot!

It's a great example of how a decorative design element could also serve a practical purpose, turning system information into something more visual and engaging.

![Jar Example](/files/pics/dev-log/12/linux-desktop-jar-example.png)

### 2. Sonar and Radar

The second direction explores radar-inspired visuals.

Using circular arcs, sweeping lines and expanding signals, the interface could provide a visual representation of network activity and discovered devices.

Network hosts could appear as echoes, with their positions potentially representing latency or distance.

Another variation incorporates the Dolphin, with expanding arcs representing signal strength and network connectivity.

This approach could give users an interesting visual overview of what's happening around their device, without relying entirely on numbers and text.

![Sonar Example](/files/pics/dev-log/12/linux-desktop-sonar-example.png)

### 3. The Ocean

The third concept takes inspiration from the aquatic identity associated with Flipper.

Here, the ocean itself becomes a visual representation of system activity.

The horizon could represent the time of day, while the water level could reflect CPU load. Changes in the waterline could also visualise network throughput.

One particularly interesting idea was to use a scrolling waterline as a miniature network activity graph, with the Dolphin interacting with the latest data.

Rather than simply displaying system statistics, the interface could tell a little visual story about what the device is doing.

![Ocean Example](/files/pics/dev-log/12/linux-desktop-ocean-example.png)

---

## From community ideas to a design direction

After reviewing the different proposals, the team decided to take the Jar concept forward, specifically version v004.

The proposed layout places a simplified Linux penguin inside a container on the left, alongside a larger clock and a grid of system statistics on the right.

The grid-based approach is particularly interesting because it could make the interface easier to develop and maintain. Individual modules could be configured to display different system parameters without requiring the entire screen to be redesigned.

There is also scope to evolve the jar itself into something a little more futuristic, potentially resembling a capsule, while developing a dedicated Dolphin illustration to give the interface its own personality.

Plans are now underway to turn the concepts into a working UI mock-up and gather feedback from the team using the actual device.

---

## Making the interface useful, not just attractive

Of course, designing something that looks good is only part of the challenge.

The interface also needs to work with real system data and provide information that is genuinely useful.

Some of the questions being explored include:

- Which system parameters should be displayed?
- How should different values and units be presented?
- How much space should each statistic occupy?
- Should icons accompany individual values?
- Which information is most important to have immediately available?

:::hint{type="note"}
There is also the possibility of making the home screen configurable, allowing users to choose which statistics they want to see.

The Dolphin could even become an interactive representation of an important system parameter, rather than simply being an illustration.
:::

---

## What's next?

The Jar concept has been selected as the direction for further development, but there is still plenty of room for refinement.

The next challenge is bringing the visual design and real system information together into something that feels unmistakably Flipper.
